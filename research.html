Research

Publications, research directions, and presentations

My research program centers on one question: how can AI tell whether information is still appropriate to use now? My dissertation develops a framework for characterizing temporal change in knowledge-grounded AI, supported by work in explainable AI, medical vision-language systems, and video understanding.

Dissertation Research

Characterizing Temporal Change for Reliable Knowledge-Grounded AI. My dissertation asks how AI systems can tell whether retrieved information is still appropriate to use now — not merely whether it is relevant or recent. Timeliness is treated as distinct from recency: older information may remain useful, and the newest information is not always the most appropriate.

Research Directions

Faithful XAI for Medical Image Report Generation

In progress

A unified evaluation framework that links visual saliency maps, model reasoning chains, and clinical correctness metrics to test whether explanations from chest X-ray report-generation models are faithful to the model's actual reasoning — not just plausible-sounding. In clinical settings, a fluent but unfaithful explanation is worse than none at all, because it can mislead radiologists. The framework scores explanations on three axes — where the model looked (saliency), how it reasoned (reasoning chains), and whether the clinical findings are correct — so unfaithful behavior can be detected and reduced before deployment.

Soccer Action Detection Using TimeSformer

Published — Journal of Computing Sciences in Colleges (ACM Digital Library), 2026

Transformer-based video understanding for context-dependent soccer actions (e.g., pass, shot, dribble) on the SoccerAct10 dataset, reaching 91.13% accuracy with divided space-time attention. The key insight is that many soccer actions are defined by game context rather than motion alone — a pass and a clearance can look identical frame-by-frame — so the model learns spatiotemporal patterns across frames. The work is documented in a 44-cell analysis notebook and a full project report. [ACM]

Explainable Image Forgery Detection

Research project

A ResNet50 + U-Net + Grad-CAM pipeline that both detects manipulated images and localizes the forged regions — 80.32% accuracy and 0.88 forged recall on CASIA 2.0. A companion multi-branch EfficientNet-B3 model adds DCT frequency-domain features, which catch compression artifacts invisible to the naked eye. Grad-CAM heatmaps overlay every decision, so each verdict is visually auditable rather than a black-box score.

NewsLogic: AI-Powered Fake News Detection

Deployed application

An end-to-end fake-news classifier combining a BERT-tiny + DistilBERT ensemble with LLaMA-4-Scout generated explanations — 83.1% accuracy and 0.91 AUC across 71 live-app cases, deployed as a FastAPI service. Each verdict comes with a natural-language rationale so users can see why an article was flagged. The project includes architecture diagrams, a final evaluation report, and a working demo app.

Fish Disease Detection with FishNet CNN

Presented — ATU Scholars Symposium, 2024

CNN-based 7-class disease detection for South Asian freshwater fish aquaculture, reaching ~92.5% test accuracy with a custom FishNet CNN, compared against a 71% transfer-learning baseline. Early automated disease detection helps small-scale farmers intervene before outbreaks spread. Presented as a 48×36 research poster.

MLBioIGE: Machine Learning and Bioinformatics for Pulmonary Fibrosis

Published — Biology Methods and Protocols, 2022

Integrated machine learning with DESeq2-based bioinformatics analysis to identify genetic effects of SARS-CoV-2 on idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis patients. The analysis spans the main cohort plus COVID, COPD, and IPF comparisons, visualized with volcano plots and PCA of differentially expressed genes. [DOI]

Programming Sequence Improvement Program (PSIP)

Published — IEEE HCIRA International Congress, 2023

A multi-year study of retention and learning outcomes across the undergraduate computer science programming sequence, built on aggregated grade data (including DFWI rates) and student surveys. The findings informed curriculum changes aimed at reducing early-course attrition, and were presented with a poster, abstract, and two presentation decks.

RAG over Soccer Video-Understanding Literature

Early-stage project

A retrieval-augmented generation playground built around a corpus of soccer video-analysis and action-recognition papers. The goal is to ask natural-language questions about the literature and get answers grounded in the actual papers, with citations back to sources. The data-ingestion layer is built — LangChain documents with rich metadata, PyMuPDF loading of a 10-paper PDF corpus — and chunking, embeddings, vector-store retrieval, and the QA loop are on the roadmap. It doubles as a testbed for the dissertation question of whether retrieved knowledge stays appropriate over time.
