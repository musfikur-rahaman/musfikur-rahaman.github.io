Research

How can AI tell whether information is still appropriate to use now?

My dissertation develops a framework for this question, supported by work in explainable AI, medical vision-language systems, and video understanding.

Dissertation · in progress

Characterizing Temporal Change for Reliable Knowledge-Grounded AI

Knowledge-grounded systems usually ask whether retrieved information is relevant or recent. My dissertation asks whether it is still appropriate to use now. Timeliness is treated as distinct from recency: older information may remain useful, and the newest information is not always the most appropriate.

Faithful XAI for Medical Image Report Generation

A survey arguing that hallucination and unfaithful explainability are two symptoms of one gap: nothing connects visual evidence, model reasoning, and output correctness. Its central claim is that the faithfulness of an explanation can serve as an early signal for hallucination, pointing toward evaluation of individual clinical statements, each linked back to image evidence.

Soccer Action Detection Using TimeSformer

Fine-tunes TimeSformer with divided space-time attention on SoccerAct10. Many soccer actions are defined by game context rather than motion alone (a pass and a clearance can look identical frame by frame), so the model learns spatiotemporal patterns across frames. Structured actions such as goal kicks and penalties score highest; fouls versus long passes remain hardest. Results are from a single training run.

Fish Disease Detection with FishNet CNN

7-class disease detection for South Asian freshwater aquaculture, helping small-scale farmers intervene before outbreaks spread. Three models were compared: a from-scratch baseline (~40%), ImageNet transfer learning (~71%), and the final tuned FishNet CNN, evaluated on 697 test images.

MLBioIGE: ML and Bioinformatics for Pulmonary Fibrosis

Why were idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis patients hit so hard by COVID-19? Using public RNA-seq data, the study ran DESeq2 differential expression across COVID-19, COPD, and IPF cohorts, built protein–protein interaction networks to surface hub genes, and mapped transcription factor–gene interactions, yielding candidate genes, pathways, and drug targets.

Programming Sequence Improvement Program (PSIP)

A multi-year study of retention across ATU’s undergraduate programming sequence, where DFWI rates (D, F, withdrawal, incomplete) ran at 32%, 35%, and 27%. Four interventions (a tutoring lab, Discord/Webex collaboration, pedagogy research, and industry-aligned curriculum) aimed to cut those rates by 10% per year. By Fall 2022 the rates were already falling.

Research project

Explainable Image Forgery Detection

A ResNet50 + U-Net + Grad-CAM pipeline that unifies classification, pixel-level localization, and visual interpretability on CASIA 2.0. In forensics a missed forgery costs more than a false alarm, so forged recall is the key metric. Grad-CAM confirms the classifier keys on genuine cues such as texture inconsistencies and splicing boundaries.

RAG over Soccer Video-Understanding Literature

A retrieval-augmented generation testbed over a 10-paper corpus on soccer action recognition and video summarization. The ingestion layer (LangChain, PyMuPDF, rich per-document metadata) is built; chunking, embeddings, retrieval, and a citation-backed QA loop are next. It doubles as a testbed for the dissertation question of whether retrieved knowledge stays appropriate over time.
