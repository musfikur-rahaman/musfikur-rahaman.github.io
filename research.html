Research

Publications, research directions, and presentations

My research program centers on one question: how can AI tell whether information is still appropriate to use now? My dissertation develops a framework for characterizing temporal change in knowledge-grounded AI, supported by work in explainable AI, medical vision-language systems, and video understanding.

Dissertation Research

Characterizing Temporal Change for Reliable Knowledge-Grounded AI. My dissertation asks how AI systems can tell whether retrieved information is still appropriate to use now — not merely whether it is relevant or recent. Timeliness is treated as distinct from recency: older information may remain useful, and the newest information is not always the most appropriate.

Research Directions

Faithful XAI for Medical Image Report Generation

Preprint — Preprints.org, 2026

A literature survey on reliability in medical AI, published as a Preprints.org preprint (2026). Its central argument: hallucination and unfaithful explainability are not separate problems but symptoms of the same gap — no mechanism connecting visual evidence, model reasoning, and output correctness. From 110 collected papers (2020–2026), 27 were selected for thematic analysis across three themes: explainability in vision transformers (attention rollout, TransLRP), medical report generation with XAI (LLaVA-Med, RaDialog, RadVLM), and hallucination detection (SelfCheckGPT, RadFlag, MedHEval). Three core findings emerged: proxy faithfulness metrics measure internal consistency, not clinical validity — a highlighted region can look meaningful without influencing the prediction at all; BLEU/ROUGE reward textual similarity, not clinical correctness; and detection operates at the output level, telling clinicians something is wrong without saying where the error originated. The survey's key claim is that faithfulness in explanation can serve as an early signal for hallucination — a model not grounded in the image will produce explanations that reveal it — pointing toward evaluation at the level of individual clinical statements, each linked back to specific image evidence. A publication-trend analysis of the 110-paper corpus found reliability work (hallucination detection, 38.2%) now slightly leading model development (31.8%) and explainability (30.0%), with sharp growth from 2024 onward — while network analysis revealed tight clustering within domains but thin bridges between them, meaning the pipeline from evidence to reasoning to verification exists only in principle. The repository includes the full 27-paper bibliography and an interactive literature navigator for browsing the surveyed work. [Preprint]

Soccer Action Detection Using TimeSformer

Published — Journal of Computing Sciences in Colleges (ACM Digital Library), 2026

Transformer-based video understanding for context-dependent soccer actions (e.g., pass, shot, dribble) on the SoccerAct10 dataset, reaching 91.13% top-1 accuracy (91.22% precision, 90.85% recall, 90.81% F1) with divided space-time attention. The key insight is that many soccer actions are defined by game context rather than motion alone — a pass and a clearance can look identical frame-by-frame — so the model learns spatiotemporal patterns across frames. Class-wise results tell the story: structured, visually distinctive actions like goal kicks (97.39 F1), penalties (96.91), and substitutions (95.24) score highest, while visually overlapping actions like fouls vs. long passes remain the hardest, as the confusion matrix shows. The model fine-tunes TimeSformer on short broadcast clips across all 10 actions — goal kick, penalty, substitution, on-target, short pass, throw-in, free kick, corner, long pass, and foul — with results reported from a single training run. A methodology diagram and an open-in-Colab notebook make the full pipeline reproducible, and the work is documented in a 44-cell analysis notebook plus a full project report. [ACM]

Explainable Image Forgery Detection

Research project

A ResNet50 + U-Net + Grad-CAM pipeline that unifies classification, pixel-level segmentation, and visual interpretability for trustworthy forensic analysis — 80.32% accuracy, 0.86 AUC, and 0.88 forged recall on CASIA 2.0. In forensics, a missed forgery is worse than a false alarm, which is why forged recall is the metric that matters most here. A companion multi-branch EfficientNet-B3 model adds DCT frequency-domain features that catch compression artifacts invisible to the naked eye. Grad-CAM heatmaps confirm the classifier keys on genuine forensic cues — texture inconsistencies, boundary artifacts, and splicing regions — so every verdict is auditable rather than a black-box score. Training dynamics are documented over 20 epochs: training loss falls from 0.56 to 0.22 while validation accuracy settles near 0.79–0.81 with AUC holding at 0.86 — mild overfitting handled by selecting the checkpoint on validation performance rather than the final epoch. Segmentation scores (Dice ~0.21, IoU ~0.15) are modest, consistent with CASIA 2.0's extremely small manipulated regions and noisy ground-truth masks.

NewsLogic: AI-Powered Fake News Detection

Deployed application

An end-to-end fake-news classifier combining a BERT-tiny + DistilBERT ensemble with LLaMA-4-Scout-generated explanations — 83.1% accuracy and 0.91 AUC across 71 live-app cases, deployed as a FastAPI service. Each verdict comes with a natural-language rationale so users can see why an article was flagged, pairing statistical classification with human-readable reasoning. The ensemble pairs two lightweight BERT variants for efficient classification with a Groq-hosted LLaMA-4-Scout for explanations — keeping inference fast enough for a live web service while giving users readable justifications instead of bare labels. The project includes architecture diagrams, a final evaluation report, and a working demo app.

Fish Disease Detection with FishNet CNN

Presented — ATU Scholars Symposium, 2024

CNN-based 7-class disease detection for South Asian freshwater fish aquaculture. Three models were built and compared: a from-scratch CNN baseline (~40%, underfit), an ImageNet-pretrained transfer-learning model (~71%), and the final tuned FishNet CNN — convolutional layers, max-pooling, dense layers, dropout, Adam optimizer — reaching 92.5% test accuracy with macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1 all at 0.93 across 697 test images. The model distinguishes 7 categories: healthy fish plus bacterial red disease, aeromoniasis, bacterial gill disease, saprolegniasis (fungal), parasitic diseases, and white tail disease (viral). Early automated detection helps small-scale farmers intervene before outbreaks spread. The project frames the problem plainly: fish farmers lose huge numbers of stock to disease every year, and spotting a sick fish still comes down to the trained human eye. Sample images, training curves, and the confusion matrix are all published alongside the notebooks, and the work was presented as a 48×36 research poster.

MLBioIGE: Machine Learning and Bioinformatics for Pulmonary Fibrosis

Published — Biology Methods and Protocols, 2022

Integrated machine learning with DESeq2-based bioinformatics analysis to answer a pressing question: why were idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis patients — people with long-term lung scarring — hit so hard by COVID-19? Using RNA-seq datasets from public gene-expression repositories, the study ran DESeq2 differential-expression analysis across COVID-19, COPD, and IPF cohorts to find shared genes and pathways, built protein–protein interaction networks to surface hub genes and functional modules, and mapped transcription factor–gene interactions to reveal the regulatory circuitry — together yielding candidate genes, pathways, and drug targets explaining how SARS-CoV-2 compounds IPF. Results are visualized with volcano plots and PCA of differentially expressed genes. The repository preserves the published paper PDF and final manuscript alongside the four analysis notebooks, keeping the full chain from public GEO data to published finding reproducible. [DOI]

Programming Sequence Improvement Program (PSIP)

Published — IEEE HCIRA International Congress, 2023

A multi-year study of retention and learning outcomes across the undergraduate computer science programming sequence. Every semester, hundreds of STEM students take ATU's programming sequence — COMS 2104 (Foundations of Programming I), COMS 2203 (Foundations of Programming II), and COMS 2213 (Data Structures) — and too many don't make it through: DFWI rates (D, F, withdrawal, incomplete) ran at 32%, 35%, and 27% across the three courses. PSIP was created to cut those rates 10% per year through four interventions: a dedicated tutoring lab, Discord/Webex collaboration spaces, pedagogy research brought into the classroom, and curriculum alignment with industry platforms like GitHub, HackerRank, and LeetCode. Built on aggregated grade data and student surveys, with DFWI rates already moving in the right direction by Fall 2022 — tracked course by course in a trend chart against the 2015–2019 baselines. The findings were presented with a poster, abstract, and two presentation decks. The repository keeps the aggregated grade data and survey instruments open for reuse.

RAG over Soccer Video-Understanding Literature

Early-stage project

A retrieval-augmented generation playground built around a corpus of soccer video-analysis and action-recognition papers. The goal is to ask natural-language questions about the literature and get answers grounded in the actual papers, with citations back to sources. The data-ingestion layer is built — LangChain documents with rich metadata, PyMuPDF loading of a 10-paper PDF corpus — and chunking, embeddings, vector-store retrieval, and the QA loop are on the roadmap. It doubles as a testbed for the dissertation question of whether retrieved knowledge stays appropriate over time. The corpus covers soccer action recognition, video classification, and sports video summarization, and every document carries rich metadata (source, page number, author, ingestion date) so future answers can cite precisely. The roadmap — chunking with overlap, embeddings in a vector store, similarity retrieval with re-ranking, and a citation-backed QA loop — is laid out openly, with the built ingestion layer clearly separated from what comes next.
