Research

Research Work, Future Directions & Publications

My research centers on computer vision and video understanding, with a major focus on soccer action detection and sports analytics. I am also interested in Explainable AI, multimodal AI systems, transformer-based deep learning, trustworthy AI, healthcare AI, and human activity recognition.

Major Research Area: Soccer Action Detection & Sports Video Understanding

Computer Vision • Sports Analytics • Transformers • Video Understanding

From Deep Learning to Transformer-Based Soccer Action Recognition

Soccer action detection is one of the central directions of my research. My work began with League of Learning: Deep Learning for Soccer Action Video Classification, where I used ConvLSTM and LRCN models to study how deep learning can recognize actions in soccer video. That work motivated me to investigate newer transformer-based approaches for modeling complex temporal relationships in video.

This progression led to my 2026 publication, Soccer Action Detection Using TimeSformer: A Transformer-Based Approach to Video Understanding. Using the SoccerAct10 dataset, the TimeSformer model achieved 91.13% validation accuracy. The study also showed that visually similar actions such as fouls, short passes, and long passes remain challenging because their meaning often depends on temporal and contextual information beyond a short clip.

  • Fine-grained soccer action recognition using deep learning and transformer-based video models.
  • Temporal reasoning for actions whose meaning depends on what happens before and after an event.
  • Sports analytics applications including event recognition, video indexing, highlight generation, and performance analysis.
  • Broader relevance to human activity recognition and complex video understanding.

Future Research Direction: Soccer Video Understanding

Over the next several years, I plan to continue building on this line of research by addressing the limitations identified in my earlier soccer-action studies and by developing more context-aware, robust, efficient, and interpretable video-understanding methods.

Longer Temporal Context

Many soccer actions cannot be interpreted reliably from only a few frames. I plan to study models that can use longer video sequences so that events such as fouls and different types of passes can be interpreted using what happens before, during, and after the action.

Multimodal & Contextual Information

I plan to investigate how transformer-based video models can be combined with contextual signals such as player pose, player movement, optical flow, field position, and potentially broadcast audio to improve recognition of ambiguous actions.

Robustness Across Broadcast Conditions

Sports footage varies across camera angles, lighting, stadium settings, zoom levels, graphics, and production styles. I plan to evaluate models across broader video conditions and explore methods that improve consistency when the testing environment differs from the original training data.

Efficiency & Near-Real-Time Analysis

Another goal is to reduce computational requirements so that advanced soccer-video models can move closer to faster or near-real-time analysis while preserving useful predictive performance.

Explainable & Trustworthy Sports AI

My work in explainable and trustworthy AI also informs this research direction. I plan to examine methods that reveal which frames, players, movements, or visual regions most strongly influence a model's prediction. This can help diagnose errors, understand model behavior, and improve reliability rather than treating classification accuracy as the only measure of success.

Broader Research Goal

Soccer will remain an important application of my research, but I view it as a practical environment for studying broader computer vision problems involving temporal reasoning, multimodal information, human activity recognition, reliability, and explainability. Methods developed through this work may therefore extend to other kinds of video where complex activities unfold over time.

Selected Research Projects

Computer Vision • Image Forensics • Explainability

Image Forgery Detection: ResNet50 + U-Net + Grad-CAM

Built an explainable computer vision pipeline for detecting and localizing image manipulation using the CASIA 2.0 dataset.

  • ResNet50 for classification and U-Net for pixel-level localization.
  • Grad-CAM for interpretable visual evidence.
  • Reported AUC: 0.86 and recall: 0.88.

Designed and deployed a full-stack AI system combining transformer-based classification, rule-based validation, and explanation generation for transparent misinformation analysis.
